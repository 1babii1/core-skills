# Client Project Discovery

Distinguish buyer, decision-maker, administrator, operator, and end user. They may want different outcomes.

## Minimum questions

- What business/user outcome should change?
- Who performs the current workflow and how?
- What happens if nothing changes?
- What is the requested launch/date constraint and why?
- Which platforms, roles, integrations, data, content, languages, and compliance rules apply?
- What must be migrated or preserved?
- Who supplies access, content, legal text, accounts, and approvals?
- Who accepts the work and by what observable criteria?
- What is explicitly excluded?
- How are discoveries and scope changes approved and priced?
- Who owns hosting, analytics, support, maintenance, and third-party fees after launch?

## Commercial safeguards

- Separate discovery estimate from delivery estimate when uncertainty is material.
- Tie milestones to accepted artifacts or outcomes, not vague completion percentages.
- Record dependencies and client-caused waiting time.
- Do not treat free revisions as undefined scope.
- State third-party availability and policy as external risk.
- Preserve a decision log and written approval for scope-changing choices.

Product discovery supports sales honesty; it must not manipulate a client into unnecessary work.
