# Evidence and Research

## Claim types

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fact | Verifiable external or project state with a source |
| Observation | Directly observed behavior or condition |
| Quote | Verbatim participant language with consent-safe attribution |
| Metric | Defined calculation over named data and time window |
| Assumption | Belief not yet supported enough for the decision |
| Inference | Interpretation derived from stated evidence |
| Decision | Chosen action, owner, date, and rationale |
| Unknown | Missing information that may change the decision |

Do not turn an inference into a fact through confident wording.

## Evidence strength

Evidence is decision-specific, but commitment usually becomes stronger in this direction:

1. opinion or hypothetical intent;
2. specific past behavior and current workaround;
3. repeated behavior across relevant participants/data;
4. commitment of time, reputation, access, or data;
5. commitment of money or contractual intent;
6. repeated successful use, retention, renewal, or realized outcome.

Negative evidence counts. Non-consumption and abandoning a workaround can be as informative as feature requests.

## Research discipline

- Define the decision and research question before collecting data.
- Recruit people who recently experienced the circumstance.
- Ask about the last concrete occurrence, alternatives, cost, and consequence.
- Separate notes from interpretation.
- Track participant segment and recruitment bias without storing unnecessary identity.
- Never claim saturation from a fixed magic number; explain what repeated and what did not.
- Triangulate interviews, behavior, support, sales, market, and technical evidence where material.
- Record disconfirming cases and alternative explanations.
