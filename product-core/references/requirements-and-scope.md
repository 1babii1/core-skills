# Requirements and Scope

## Requirement layers

1. Outcome and problem.
2. Actors, roles, permissions, and contexts.
3. Jobs, journeys, states, and business rules.
4. Functional requirements.
5. Data, integrations, migration, and lifecycle.
6. Non-functional requirements: security, privacy, accessibility, performance, reliability, observability, localization, support.
7. Acceptance criteria, edge cases, and recovery.
8. In scope, out of scope, deferred, dependencies, and open questions.

Keep product requirements implementation-neutral unless a constraint truly requires a technology.

## Scope contract

For each deliverable capture:

- observable result;
- included actors, platforms, flows, states, integrations, and content;
- explicit exclusions;
- client/user-provided inputs and deadlines;
- assumptions used for estimation;
- acceptance method and approver;
- revision/change-request mechanism;
- dependency and third-party risk;
- analytics and post-release responsibility.

An MVP is the smallest coherent test of value, not the maximum list that fits a deadline. Slice vertically through a real user outcome.

## Handoff readiness

Do not hand off unresolved questions that radically change architecture, legal exposure, data ownership, core workflow, integration availability, or cost as ordinary implementation details. Convert them into discovery tasks, spikes, or explicitly accepted risks.
