# Operating Model

## Mode differences

| Concern | Client project | New product | Existing product |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary decision | What outcome and scope are being purchased? | Is the problem and demand worth investment? | What causes the observed outcome and what should change? |
| Strong inputs | Buyer/user interviews, process evidence, constraints | Behavior, alternatives, commitments, channel access | Events, funnels, cohorts, support, revenue, interviews |
| Main danger | Building the buyer's wording instead of the user's need | Mistaking enthusiasm for demand | Mistaking correlation or aggregate averages for causation |
| Minimum artifact | Brief + scope + acceptance + risks | Problem + assumptions + validation | Diagnosis + hypothesis + measurement |

## Workflow

1. State the decision to be made and who owns it.
2. Inventory supplied evidence and unknowns.
3. Choose discovery depth based on cost of error, reversibility, novelty, and risk.
4. Frame the problem before solution detail.
5. Identify and prioritize assumptions.
6. Choose the cheapest test that can change the decision.
7. Specify only evidence-supported scope.
8. Define measurement before implementation.
9. Hand off to implementation planning.
10. Review actual outcomes at a pre-agreed date.

## Stop conditions

Pause or reduce investment when the target user cannot be identified, evidence is entirely hypothetical, no reachable channel exists, the economics cannot plausibly work, required data/authorization is unavailable, or success cannot be distinguished from failure.
