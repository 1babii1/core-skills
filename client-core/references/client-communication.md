# Client Communication

## Communication contract

Agree early on:

- primary channel and expected response window;
- status cadence and meeting purpose;
- decision-maker and acceptance owner;
- where scope, decisions, and approvals are recorded;
- escalation path for blockers, security, payment, and production incidents;
- what counts as an urgent request.

Do not imply continuous availability without an explicit paid support arrangement.

## Status update

Use a short, evidence-based structure:

1. **Outcome:** overall state and what changed since the prior update.
2. **Completed:** demonstrable results, not activity volume.
3. **Next:** near-term work and expected decision/demonstration.
4. **Client inputs:** item, owner, required date, and consequence of delay.
5. **Risks/issues:** current impact, mitigation, and decision needed.
6. **Commercial changes:** proposed scope/date/price effect, still unapproved unless accepted.

Use green/yellow/red only with explicit definitions. Do not hide a material risk to make a report look healthy.

## Meeting summary

After a substantive call capture:

- attendees and roles;
- client statements versus confirmed decisions;
- decisions and their rationale;
- action items, owners, and dates;
- open questions and assumptions;
- scope, estimate, deadline, or risk changes;
- exact next mutual step.

Draft the external recap separately from internal risk notes. Do not send it without authorization.

## Scope change

When a request may be out of scope:

1. acknowledge the desired outcome;
2. compare it to the agreed baseline;
3. state why it is a defect, clarification, or change;
4. explain impact on deliverables, dependencies, risk, price, and calendar;
5. offer options: swap scope, add work, defer, investigate, or decline;
6. record approval before starting the changed work.

Avoid accusatory “scope creep” language. Make the decision visible and commercial.

## Difficult conversations

### Delay or estimate change

State the observed cause, affected commitment, current evidence, options, recommendation, and next decision date. Do not conceal uncertainty until the deadline.

### Payment delay

Reference the agreed invoice/milestone and due state, ask whether there is an administrative or substantive dispute, state the applicable next step from the agreement, and avoid threats not supported by the contract or law.

### Quality concern

Reproduce the issue, compare it to acceptance, separate defect from enhancement, define correction and verification, and communicate ownership and timing.

### Declining work

Be brief and respectful. State the mismatch without diagnosing the client. When appropriate, suggest a safer scope or referral without exposing confidential information.

### Production incident

Route to `delivery-core` and incident procedures. Stabilize and verify before debating blame or commercial consequences.

Silence, meeting attendance, or failure to object is not approval unless the applicable agreement and law clearly make it so.
