# Proposal and SOW

## Choose the document

- **Estimate/quote:** price and conditions for a sufficiently understood request.
- **Proposal:** helps the buyer decide whether the approach and engagement fit.
- **Statement of Work (SOW):** defines the operational scope under an agreement.
- **Discovery proposal:** sells a bounded learning outcome before implementation.

Do not overload a small order with enterprise ceremony. Do not compress a risky project into a one-line price.

## Proposal readiness gate

A delivery proposal requires enough evidence to state:

- who the prospective client is and why the work is relevant;
- the client-confirmed outcome or request;
- the scope basis and material unknowns;
- the deliverable and acceptance approach;
- an estimate basis for price and calendar;
- confirmed user capacity for any promised availability.

If these are missing, do not simulate a proposal. Prepare one of:

- a concise, truthful first-contact message;
- a short list of qualification questions;
- an invitation to an exploratory call;
- a bounded discovery proposal whose own scope, price, and timing are confirmed.

## Evidence-first proposal

Use only confirmed client statements, sourced facts, documented observations, and explicitly labeled assumptions.

Never include:

- fabricated or anonymized-looking case studies that did not happen;
- testimonials without permission;
- ROI or savings based on invented inputs;
- guaranteed revenue, ranking, security, approval, or platform outcome;
- artificial urgency, expiring capacity, or discount;
- an implementation date unsupported by the estimate;
- a technical solution presented as certain before material feasibility is tested.

If proof is limited, state that honestly and reduce risk with a smaller first stage, prototype, milestone, warranty boundary, or transparent process evidence.

Do not replace one unsupported commitment with another. A promised audit, discovery, prototype, or implementation date requires confirmed user availability and an estimate basis. If identity, price, availability, or timeline is missing, produce a clearly marked draft plus the exact fields that must be confirmed before sending.

Before research or conversation, do not write “we noticed,” “you are losing,” “your users struggle,” or similar statements as facts. Frame an area as the developer's relevant capability or a hypothesis to investigate.

A deadline requested by the prospect or user is a constraint to validate, not availability to promise. Do not include it in an external-ready draft as a commitment, target, possibility, or conditional launch date until scope, dependencies, estimate, and user capacity support it.

## Minimum proposal structure

1. **Context:** concise understanding of the request and desired outcome.
2. **Proposed engagement:** approach and why it fits the current evidence.
3. **Deliverables:** observable artifacts or working outcomes.
4. **Scope:** included flows, roles, platforms, content, integrations, data, environments, and release work.
5. **Exclusions and deferred items:** explicit boundaries.
6. **Assumptions and dependencies:** client, developer, and third-party.
7. **Acceptance:** evidence, approver, review window, and defect versus change distinction.
8. **Plan:** milestones, demonstrations, decisions, and calendar range.
9. **Commercials:** model, price/rate, currency, expenses, taxes/fees to verify, payment cadence, and validity.
10. **Changes:** how scope, price, and dates are revised.
11. **Post-launch:** warranty, support, maintenance, hosting, analytics, ownership, and handoff boundary.
12. **Risks and unknowns:** material uncertainty and proposed validation.
13. **Next step:** one concrete mutual action.

## SOW alignment

For every deliverable, record:

| Field | Required content |
| --- | --- |
| Result | What will exist or work |
| Included | Actors, flows, states, platforms, integrations, content |
| Excluded | Clear non-deliverables |
| Inputs | Client-provided access, decisions, content, accounts |
| Acceptance | Test/evidence, approver, review period |
| Dependencies | External services, policies, availability |
| Milestone | Demonstration or delivery point |
| Commercial | Price/rate treatment and payment trigger |
| Change trigger | What requires a revised estimate or order |

Keep requirements in `product-core`; keep commercial consequences and document consistency here.

## Options

Offer alternatives only when they represent real tradeoffs, for example:

- discovery versus delivery;
- narrow launch versus broader scope;
- fixed price versus time and materials;
- implementation only versus launch and handoff;
- essential versus optional integration.

Do not create three artificial tiers merely to anchor the buyer. For each option explain outcome, exclusions, uncertainty, price model, and suitable use.

## Consistency review

Cross-check the proposal, estimate, product scope, acceptance criteria, agreement, and CRM:

- same deliverables and exclusions;
- same client responsibilities;
- same price, currency, milestones, and validity;
- same calendar assumptions;
- same acceptance and change process;
- same IP, third-party, support, and handoff boundary;
- no unresolved placeholder or conflicting version.

Mark the document `Draft` until the user approves external use. Submission, upload, sharing, and signature are separate authorized actions.
