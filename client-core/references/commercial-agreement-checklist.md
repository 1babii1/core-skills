# Commercial Agreement Checklist

## Purpose and legal boundary

Use this checklist to surface missing decisions and negotiation risks. It is not a universal contract template or legal opinion.

Before making jurisdiction-specific claims, identify:

- legal names and status of the parties;
- countries/regions and intended governing law;
- consumer versus business relationship;
- data, regulated domain, subcontractors, and cross-border activity;
- deal value, duration, and potential downside.

Verify current law, tax, sanctions, platform, payment, and registration requirements from primary official sources. Escalate material, novel, regulated, cross-border, employment-like, IP-heavy, or high-liability matters to qualified counsel.

## Core checklist

### Parties and authority

- Correct legal identities and contact/notice mechanism.
- Signer authority and effective date.
- Independent contractor relationship and subcontracting position.

### Services and scope

- Agreement and SOW hierarchy.
- Deliverables, exclusions, milestones, environments, and documentation.
- Client inputs, access, content, decisions, accounts, and deadlines.
- Third-party systems, availability, policy, and fees.

### Price and payment

- Currency, amount/rate, invoicing trigger, due date, and payment method.
- Taxes, withholding, transfer, marketplace, and currency-conversion treatment to verify.
- Deposit or first milestone condition.
- Late payment, suspension, restart, collection, and disputed-invoice process.
- Expenses and pre-approval.
- Treatment of cancellation and work in progress.

Do not prescribe a universal deposit percentage, interest rate, or tax treatment.

### Acceptance and changes

- Observable acceptance evidence and named approver.
- Review period and a reasonable cure/retest path.
- Defect definition versus new request.
- Written change request describing scope, price, date, and dependency impact.
- Priority and work-start rule for an unapproved change.

### Intellectual property and materials

- Client pre-existing materials and rights assurance.
- Developer pre-existing tools, reusable know-how, libraries, and templates.
- Ownership or license for project-specific work and the payment condition for transfer.
- Open-source and third-party license obligations.
- Portfolio/publicity permission, confidentiality exceptions, and attribution.
- Domain, hosting, repositories, accounts, analytics, and app-store ownership.

Do not assume “work for hire” or a broad assignment works identically across jurisdictions.

### Confidentiality, data, and security

- Definition, exclusions, permitted recipients, duration, and return/deletion.
- Controller/processor or analogous roles only after actual processing is understood.
- Data categories, purpose, location, subprocessors, retention, incidents, and assistance.
- Security commitments limited to defined, achievable controls.
- No promise of absolute security or regulatory compliance.

### Warranties, support, and operations

- Conformance warranty or defect-correction boundary.
- Warranty duration and start condition.
- Excluded causes: client changes, unsupported environments, third parties, misuse.
- Maintenance, support hours, response targets, SLA, hosting, backups, and monitoring.
- Emergency work, incidents, and additional rates where relevant.

### Risk allocation

- Representations and warranties.
- Liability cap, exclusions, carveouts, and symmetry.
- Indemnity subject, procedure, defense control, and relationship to the cap.
- Insurance only if available and proportionate.
- Force majeure and dependency failures.

The correct allocation depends on jurisdiction, bargaining position, deal economics, data, and potential harm. Flag; do not invent “market standard.”

### Term, suspension, and exit

- Term, renewal, notice, and termination for breach/convenience.
- Cure period where appropriate.
- Payment and deliverables upon exit.
- Transition assistance, data export/deletion, credential/access transfer, and survival.
- Governing law, venue, escalation, mediation/arbitration/litigation to review.

## Negotiation record

For each issue record:

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Clause | Location and topic |
| Current position | Neutral summary, with exact text retained in the working document |
| Concern | Practical consequence for this engagement |
| Evidence | Playbook, official source, counsel, or commercial decision |
| Preferred outcome | What to request |
| Fallback | Acceptable alternative if authorized |
| Authority | Who can accept residual risk |
| Status | Open, agreed, escalated, accepted risk |

Never claim a redline was accepted, send it, or sign anything without explicit authorization and verification.

## Start gate

Before meaningful implementation confirm:

1. Essential terms and applicable SOW are accepted through the chosen mechanism.
2. Initial payment condition is satisfied or explicitly waived by the user.
3. Scope, acceptance, client responsibilities, and change process match the proposal.
4. Material IP, data, security, third-party, support, and exit questions are resolved or accepted by the right owner.
5. Required access is provided through safe mechanisms without exposing secrets to the agent.
