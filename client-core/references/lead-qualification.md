# Lead Qualification

## Decide, do not decorate

Qualification exists to choose the next investment:

- decline;
- ask one or more focused questions;
- conduct a short exploratory call;
- offer paid discovery or a spike;
- prepare an estimate;
- prepare a proposal;
- refer to another specialist.

Do not award confidence from a large scoring framework when the underlying facts are absent.

## Minimum qualification record

Capture what is confirmed and what remains unknown:

| Area | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| Outcome | What should change for the business or users? Why now? |
| Current state | How is the task handled today and what fails? |
| Buyer | Who wants the result, controls the budget, and signs? |
| Acceptance | Who decides the work is accepted, using what evidence? |
| Users | Who operates, administers, and is affected by the result? |
| Scope drivers | Platforms, roles, integrations, data, migration, content, languages, compliance |
| Constraints | Deadline and reason, budget constraint, required stack, geography, procurement |
| Client inputs | Access, content, accounts, policies, subject knowledge, approvals |
| Commercial fit | Payment path, communication cadence, decision speed, future ownership |
| Delivery fit | Capability, capacity, dependencies, legal/ethical risk, support expectation |

Unknown budget is not automatic disqualification. A refusal to discuss any constraint after meaningful discovery is a risk.

## Evidence discipline

Public research can reveal facts such as products, locations, public hiring, published technology, or announced initiatives. It cannot prove private pain, budget, authority, intent, or urgency.

Use signals to form questions:

- Good: “Your public careers page shows two operations roles. Is reducing manual work part of this project?”
- Bad: “You are hiring, so you have budget and urgently need automation.”

Record the source and date for unstable claims. Avoid restricted scraping and sensitive personal profiling.

## Fit and risk review

Assess without pretending to know an exact probability:

- **Problem fit:** clear, meaningful, and addressable.
- **Client fit:** responsive, respectful, able to decide and contribute.
- **Capability fit:** within the stack and skill boundary.
- **Commercial fit:** plausible budget/payment path and acceptable opportunity cost.
- **Delivery fit:** reasonable dependencies, timeline, acceptance, and ownership.
- **Risk:** legal, ethical, security, data, third-party, reputation, nonpayment, or scope volatility.

Use `Strong`, `Mixed`, `Weak`, or `Unknown` with evidence. A hard disqualifier overrides an attractive total.

## Red flags

Pause or decline when there is:

- illegality, deception, plagiarism, malware, evasion, fake engagement, or abusive data use;
- a request to hide material facts from users, platforms, partners, or regulators;
- pressure to use someone else's accounts, credentials, identity, licenses, or content;
- an impossible deadline paired with refusal to reduce scope;
- undefined deliverables plus demand for fixed price and unlimited revisions;
- no identifiable decision-maker or acceptance owner;
- refusal to put essential commitments in writing;
- payment dependent entirely on an outcome outside the developer's control;
- repeated disrespect, urgency manipulation, or attempts to obtain finished work as a “test”;
- a business model or client whose reputational risk is unacceptable.

Do not diagnose fraud from one weak signal. Verify, narrow the engagement, request safer terms, or decline.

## Discovery boundary

A short introductory call may establish fit, constraints, and next steps. It should not silently include architecture, complete requirements, production design, a detailed implementation plan, or a reusable solution.

Recommend paid discovery when:

- multiple workflows or stakeholders remain unclear;
- architecture depends on unknown integrations or data;
- migration, security, legal, or compliance exposure is material;
- a prototype or spike is needed to test feasibility;
- the client needs a decision artifact even if implementation goes elsewhere.

Route product truth and detailed scope to `product-core`. Return to `client-core` with a commercial decision and estimate basis.
