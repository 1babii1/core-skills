# Art Direction Modes

Choose one dominant mode. These are decision frames, not preset styles.

## Quiet editorial or architectural

Use for architecture, culture, high-trust services, thoughtful portfolios, and content where work must speak.

- Build rhythm with negative space, measured image scale, calm type, and precise alignment.
- Let a few small labels contrast with one strong image or statement.
- Use restrained transitions and avoid decorative UI chrome.
- Make mobile denser but not cramped; preserve reading measure and image priority.
- Risk: appearing unfinished. Counter with exact typography, crop, spacing, and detail.

## Product and material luxury

Use when finish, craft, texture, engineering, or physical presence is central.

- Define material, roughness, reflection, translucency, edge treatment, environment, and light direction.
- Show a single hero object before a catalogue of details.
- Use shadow and depth to communicate shape, not to decorate every card.
- Pair tactile imagery with quiet controls and clear product information.
- Risk: slow or empty loading. Provide a strong poster and lazy enhancement.

## Bold typographic identity

Use when the voice or name is the main asset and content density is low.

- Treat words as composition through scale, line breaks, contrast, crop, rhythm, and type pairing.
- Keep decorative/accent faces for short text; preserve readable body typography.
- Combine macro type with micro-labels only when contrast and hierarchy remain clear.
- Design line breaks at narrow widths instead of uniformly shrinking desktop type.
- Risk: accidental overflow or illegibility. Test real copy in target languages.

## Tactile organic surrealism

Use for youth, fashion, culture, creative education, and playful product worlds.

- Contrast soft or absurd forms with a hard typographic/grid system.
- Specify physical material: fur, clay, silicone, gel, rubber, liquid, foam, or textile.
- Use one surprising proportion or transformation as the memorable move.
- Keep purchase, navigation, sizing, and product facts conventional enough to use.
- Risk: generic AI surrealism. Anchor the object in a brand-specific metaphor.

## Technical or cyber system

Use for technology, AI, tools, labs, games, and experimental portfolios.

- Use grids, monospaced labels, coordinates, system notation, scanlines, grain, or glitches as a consistent language.
- Preserve deliberate hierarchy beneath apparent disorder.
- Treat “errors” as authored states, never as an excuse for broken interaction.
- Keep long reading text calmer than display text.
- Risk: trend sameness and fatigue. Choose one system metaphor and remove the rest.

## Immersive spatial or 3D

Use when spatial inspection, configuration, demonstration, or brand theatre justifies interaction cost.

- Give the user an obvious objective and constrained controls.
- Let the object guide narrative progression rather than float decoratively.
- Synchronize DOM copy and 3D state through one data model.
- Provide progress, skip, pause where required, and a meaningful static form.
- Risk: turning a website into an unexplained tech demo. Keep UX and action visible.

## Cinematic image-led presentation

Use for real estate, interiors, automotive, hospitality, events, and campaigns where atmosphere sells.

- Art-direct crop, focal point, overlay, and copy placement together.
- Use full-bleed media selectively; alternate intensity with quieter sections.
- Preserve subject focal points across responsive crops.
- Avoid carousels when a single decisive image tells the story better.
- Risk: generic brochure design. Add a specific typographic, navigational, or narrative signature.

## Combining modes

Combine only through a shared thesis:

- quiet editorial + material luxury: “objects treated as exhibits”;
- technical system + organic surrealism: “machine precision made human”;
- bold typography + cinematic imagery: “language interrupts the scene”;
- immersive 3D + product luxury: “inspect before purchase.”

Do not combine three modes unless the page is explicitly episodic and each chapter has a stable navigation and reading model.
